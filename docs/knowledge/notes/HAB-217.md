### 2026-08-11

**Research: validated the 6-item checklist against external release-notes guidance, added a 7th item**

Ran `/research` (topic mode) against the draft `user-bullet-checklist.md` before finalizing it. Findings:

| Source | Quality | Bears on |
|---|---|---|
| [Google Play Console Help — Prepare and roll out a release](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859348?hl=en) | Official platform policy | Surfaced a gap — see below |
| [NN/G — Plain Language Is for Everyone, Even Experts](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/plain-language-experts/) | Established UX research authority | Backs items 1, 3, 4 |
| [UX Collective — The Art of Writing Great Release Notes](https://uxdesign.cc/the-art-of-writing-great-release-notes-6607e22efae1) | Practitioner blog, not policy | Backs items 2, 3, 5 |
| [Google Developer Style Guide — "What's new" page](https://developers.google.com/style/whats-new) | Official, but structural example only | Weak fit, item 6 loosely |

No official Apple guidance on What's New copy could be found or verified despite two focused searches — flagged as a gap rather than filled with a guess.

The original 6 items (understandable, cared-about, no jargon, plain language, right altitude of detail, currently-true) all held up against the evidence — no contradictions. One real gap: Google Play's own policy explicitly bans promotional/CTA content in release notes and enforces a 500-char-per-language limit, and nothing in the original 6 items would have caught a bullet like "Check out our exciting new break feature — try it today!" Added **item 7 — no promotional/CTA language** — to `skills/manage/draft-release-notes/resources/user-bullet-checklist.md`, with a worked ❌/✅ example.
