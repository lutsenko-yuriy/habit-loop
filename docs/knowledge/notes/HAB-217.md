### 2026-08-11

**Research: validated the 6-item checklist against external release-notes guidance, added a 7th item**

Ran `/research` (topic mode) against the draft `user-bullet-checklist.md` before finalizing it. Findings:

| Source | Quality | Bears on |
|---|---|---|
| [Google Play Console Help — Prepare and roll out a release](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859348?hl=en) | Official platform policy | Surfaced a gap — see below |
| [NN/G — Plain Language Is for Everyone, Even Experts](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/plain-language-experts/) | Established UX research authority | Backs items 1, 3, 4 |
| [UX Collective — The Art of Writing Great Release Notes](https://uxdesign.cc/the-art-of-writing-great-release-notes-6607e22efae1) | Practitioner blog, not policy | Backs items 2, 3, 5 |
| [Google Developer Style Guide — "What's new" page](https://developers.google.com/style/whats-new) | Official, but structural example only | Weak fit, item 6 loosely |

No official Apple guidance on What's New copy could be found or verified despite two focused searches — flagged as a gap rather than filled with a guess.

The original 6 items (understandable, cared-about, no jargon, plain language, right altitude of detail, currently-true) all held up against the evidence — no contradictions. One real gap: Google Play's own policy explicitly bans promotional/CTA content in release notes and enforces a 500-char-per-language limit, and nothing in the original 6 items would have caught a bullet like "Check out our exciting new break feature — try it today!" Added **item 7 — no promotional/CTA language** — to `skills/manage/draft-release-notes/resources/user-bullet-checklist.md`, with a worked ❌/✅ example.

### 2026-08-11 (2)

**Process observation for debrief: a feature ticket absorbed a small ad-hoc research step mid-flight**

HAB-217 is a feature ticket (FEATURE_WORKFLOW), not a research ticket (RESEARCH_WORKFLOW) — but partway through implementation (after the PR-worthy design was already settled, closer to review than to `/brief`) the user asked to validate the draft checklist against external release-notes guidance, and `/research` was invoked inline to do it (see the entry above). This wasn't ticket-scale research — no ticket-specific constraints pass, no MoSCoW, no ADR — just a bounded, single-topic sanity check that happened to reuse the `research` skill's citation discipline. It landed as a note in this file rather than a standalone research ticket.

Worth discussing at debrief: should a small, bounded research check like this have a lighter-weight, explicitly sanctioned path when it surfaces *during* a feature ticket (e.g. as an optional `brief`-step-style gate, the way `brief` step 3.5 has an optional visual-alignment gate) rather than only existing as (a) a full separate RESEARCH_WORKFLOW ticket or (b) an ungated ad hoc `/research` call wherever someone thinks to make one? This ticket's case worked fine, but the pattern isn't written down anywhere — the next person to want this might not think to reach for `/research` mid-feature at all.

## Debrief summary

### 2026-08-11

**What went well**
- Overall a smooth ticket. Live review/audit passes caught and fixed real bugs immediately — the internal-only-WU skip missing from ship's new step 2, and duplicate `gh pr diff` fetches — both landing in the "optimize Linear/tool round-trips" lineage started in HAB-176, which is now visibly paying off in reduced token consumption on every Linear-touching skill.
- The mid-ticket `/research` check (see the notes entries above) worked well as a bounded, cited sanity pass on the drafted checklist, catching a real gap (no promotional/CTA rule) that internal review alone hadn't surfaced.

**What was hard or surprising**
- The local `skill_router` (LM Studio) path for `/implement` silently gutted `skills/manage/ship/SKILL.md` from 142 to 33 lines by rewriting the whole file instead of editing it, only caught because the diff was checked before trusting the result. The router did exit non-zero (triggering the documented fallback), but nothing in the fallback convention said to check for already-written damage before falling back — this ticket's session did that as ordinary care, not because a rule said to.

**What to change**
- Formalized the ad hoc `/research` step this ticket used as a first-class, nameable planning unit: **Research-WU** (optional, proposed by `plan` when a design choice would benefit from external validation; omitted when the ticket is genuinely novel with no precedent to research). Always sequenced before the WU that follows it.
- Renamed the two existing WU0 variants for consistency with the above: **Scenario-WU** (today's `draft-scenarios`/`AppHarness` case) and **Checklist-WU** (today's verification-checklist case). All three (Research-WU, Scenario-WU, Checklist-WU) are named, not numbered — everything after them keeps the existing WU1/WU2/… numeric naming unchanged.
- Added a safeguard to the `skill_router` fallback convention: before spawning the fallback Agent, check `git status`/`git diff --stat` for damage the failed local-model run may have already written, and revert it so the fallback starts clean.

**Files changed:** `docs/FEATURE_WORKFLOW.md` (Research-WU/Scenario-WU/Checklist-WU naming), `skills/design/plan/SKILL.md` (WU rules mirror the same convention), `skills/configure/calibrate/resources/stub-formats.md` (fallback safeguard).
